Assumptions:

  • negligible influence of the Sun and other planets: Earth-Moon system is a two-body problem;
    • the center of mass of the system can be set as the origin of an inertial reference frame
    • the orbit is planar
  • negligible eccentricity: circular orbit (constant radius and angular velocity)

1 Kinematics

Let \(B\) be the center of mass (CM) of the Earth-Moon system, which we assume is an inertial reference frame, and let’s introduce some useful reference frames:

  • Frame \((0)\): Inertial frame centered at \(B\) with axes \((\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0, \hat{\mathbf{y}}_0, \hat{\mathbf{z}}_0)\).
  • Frame \((1)\): Centered at \(B\), rotating with the orbital angular velocity of the Earth-Moon system \(\theta_1(t)\) around \(\hat{\mathbf{z}}_0\). The angular velocity of \((1)\) w.r.t. \((0)\) is constant \(\Omega_1 = \dot{\theta}_1\) is the orbit is circular.
  • Frame \((2)\): Centered at the Earth’s center \(E\), tilted by the obliquity angle \(\varepsilon\) around \(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_0\) (or \(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_1\)). The obliquity is assumed to be constant.
  • Frame \((3)\): Centered at \(E\), rotating with the Earth’s daily rotation \(\theta_3(t)\) around \(\hat{\mathbf{z}}_2\). The rotation angular velocity of the Earth around its axis \(\Omega_3 = \dot{\theta}_3\) is assumed to be constant.

NoteChange of bases

  1. \(0 \to 1\): Rotation by \(\theta_1(t)\) around \(\hat{\mathbf{z}}_0\) \[ \begin{cases} \hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 = \hat{\mathbf{x}}_0 \cos\theta_1 + \hat{\mathbf{y}}_0 \sin\theta_1 \\ \hat{\mathbf{y}}_1 = -\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0 \sin\theta_1 + \hat{\mathbf{y}}_0 \cos\theta_1 \\ \hat{\mathbf{z}}_1 = \hat{\mathbf{z}}_0 \end{cases} \]

  2. \(0 \to 2\): Tilt by \(\varepsilon\) around \(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_0\) \[ \begin{cases} \hat{\mathbf{x}}_2 = \hat{\mathbf{x}}_0 \cos\varepsilon - \hat{\mathbf{z}}_0 \sin\varepsilon \\ \hat{\mathbf{y}}_2 = \hat{\mathbf{y}}_0 \\ \hat{\mathbf{z}}_2 = \hat{\mathbf{x}}_0 \sin\varepsilon + \hat{\mathbf{z}}_0 \cos\varepsilon \end{cases} \]

  3. \(2 \to 3\): Rotation by \(\theta_3(t)\) around \(\hat{\mathbf{z}}_2\) \[ \begin{cases} \hat{\mathbf{x}}_3 = \hat{\mathbf{x}}_2 \cos\theta_3 + \hat{\mathbf{y}}_2 \sin\theta_3 \\ \hat{\mathbf{y}}_3 = -\hat{\mathbf{x}}_2 \sin\theta_3 + \hat{\mathbf{y}}_2 \cos\theta_3 \\ \hat{\mathbf{z}}_3 = \hat{\mathbf{z}}_2 \end{cases} \]

NoteVelocity and acceleration of the center of the Earth \((E)\) w.r.t. the center of mass \((B)\)

The angular velocity of frame \((3)\) with respect to the inertial frame \((0)\) is: \[ \boldsymbol{\omega}_{3/0} = \dot{\theta}_3 \hat{\mathbf{z}}_2 \]

Let \(R_{E,1}\) be the distance from the barycenter \(B\) to the Earth’s center \(E\). The velocity and acceleration of \(E\) with respect to frame \((0)\) are: \[ \mathbf{v}^{(0)}_{E/B} = R_{E,1} \dot{\theta}_1 \hat{\mathbf{y}}_1 \] \[ \mathbf{a}^{(0)}_{E/B} = -R_{E,1} \dot{\theta}_1^2 \hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 \]

From the equation of motion of the Earth around the barycenter (due to the Moon’s gravity): \[ -M_E R_{E,1} \dot{\theta}_1^2 = -\frac{G M_E M_M}{R_{EM}^2} \implies \dot{\theta}_1^2 = \frac{G M_M}{R_{EM}^2 R_{E,1}} \] Therefore, the acceleration of the Earth’s center is: \[ \mathbf{a}^{(0)}_{E/B} = -\frac{G M_M}{R_{EM}^2} \hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 \]

2 Relative Dynamics in Earth fram Earth frame

For a point mass \(m\) at position \(P\) on the Earth’s surface, Newton’s second law in the inertial frame is: \[ m \mathbf{a}^{(0)}_{P/B} = \mathbf{F} \]

Using relative kinematics between frame \((0)\) and frame \((3)\): \[ \mathbf{a}^{(0)}_{P/B} = \mathbf{a}^{(0)}_{E/B} + \mathbf{a}^{(3)}_{P/E} + \boldsymbol{\alpha}_{3/0} \times \mathbf{r}_{EP} + 2\boldsymbol{\omega}_{3/0} \times \mathbf{v}^{(3)}_{P/E} + \boldsymbol{\omega}_{3/0} \times (\boldsymbol{\omega}_{3/0} \times \mathbf{r}_{EP}) \]

Solving for the acceleration \(\mathbf{a}^{(3)}_{P/E}\) observed in the Earth-fixed frame \((3)\): \[ \mathbf{a}^{(3)}_{P/E} = \frac{\mathbf{F}}{m} - \mathbf{a}^{(0)}_{E/B} - \boldsymbol{\alpha}_{3/0} \times \mathbf{r}_{EP} - 2\boldsymbol{\omega}_{3/0} \times \mathbf{v}^{(3)}_{P/E} - \boldsymbol{\omega}_{3/0} \times (\boldsymbol{\omega}_{3/0} \times \mathbf{r}_{EP}) \]

2.1 Real Forces Acting on \(P\)

  1. Gravitational force from the Earth: \[ \mathbf{F}_E = -\frac{G M_E m}{R_E^2} \hat{\mathbf{R}}_P \] where \(\hat{\mathbf{R}}_P\) is the unit radial vector from the center of the Earth \(E\) to \(P\).

  2. Gravitational force from the Moon: Since the vector from the Moon to \(P\) is \(R_{EM}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 + R_E\hat{\mathbf{R}}_P\): \[ \mathbf{F}_M = -G M_M m \frac{R_{EM}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 + R_E\hat{\mathbf{R}}_P}{|R_{EM}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 + R_E\hat{\mathbf{R}}_P|^3} = -\frac{G M_M m}{R_{EM}^2} \frac{\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 + \frac{R_E}{R_{EM}}\hat{\mathbf{R}}_P}{\left|\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 + \frac{R_E}{R_{EM}}\hat{\mathbf{R}}_P\right|^3} \] Using a first-order Taylor expansion for \(\frac{R_E}{R_{EM}} \ll 1\): \[ \mathbf{F}_M \approx -\frac{G M_M m}{R_{EM}^2} \left\{ \hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 + \frac{R_E}{R_{EM}} \left( \hat{\mathbf{R}}_P - 3(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 \cdot \hat{\mathbf{R}}_P)\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 \right) \right\} \]

  3. Non-gravitational forces (\(\mathbf{F}_{\text{non-g}}\)): Pressure gradients (\(\nabla P\)) in fluids, friction, etc.

2.2 Combined Equation of Motion

Substituting \(\mathbf{F}_E\), \(\mathbf{F}_M\), and \(\mathbf{a}^{(0)}_{E/B}\) into the relative acceleration equation (noting that the \(-\frac{G M_M}{R_{EM}^2}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1\) terms cancel out): \[ \mathbf{a}^{(3)}_{P/E} \approx -\frac{G M_E}{R_E^2} \hat{\mathbf{R}}_P - \frac{G M_M}{R_{EM}^2} \frac{R_E}{R_{EM}} \left( \hat{\mathbf{R}}_P - 3(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 \cdot \hat{\mathbf{R}}_P)\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 \right) - \boldsymbol{\alpha}_{3/0} \times \mathbf{r}_{EP} - 2\boldsymbol{\omega}_{3/0} \times \mathbf{v}^{(3)}_{P/E} - \boldsymbol{\omega}_{3/0} \times (\boldsymbol{\omega}_{3/0} \times \mathbf{r}_{EP}) + \frac{\mathbf{F}_{\text{non-g}}}{m} \]

2.2.1 Assumptions & Orders of Magnitude

  • Assumptions: Earth’s rotation rate is constant (\(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{3/0} = \mathbf{0}\) and \(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{3/0} = \Omega_E \hat{\mathbf{z}}_2\)).
  • Constants:
    • \(G \approx 6.67 \times 10^{-11} \text{ m}^3 \text{kg}^{-1} \text{s}^{-2}\)
    • \(M_E \approx 5.97 \times 10^{24} \text{ kg}\), \(M_M \approx 7.35 \times 10^{22} \text{ kg}\)
    • \(R_{EM} \approx 3.84 \times 10^8 \text{ m}\), \(R_E \approx 6.3 \times 10^6 \text{ m}\)
    • \(\Omega_E \approx \frac{1 \text{ rad}}{1 \text{ day}} \approx 1.1 \times 10^{-5} \text{ s}^{-1}\) (or \(2\pi / 86400 \approx 7.27 \times 10^{-5} \text{ rad/s}\))
  • Accelerations:
    • Earth’s surface gravity: \(g_0 = \frac{G M_E}{R_E^2} \approx 10 \text{ m/s}^2\)
    • Tidal acceleration scale: \(\frac{G M_M}{R_{EM}^2} \frac{R_E}{R_{EM}} \approx (4 \times 10^{-5}) \times (2 \times 10^{-2}) \approx 8 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m/s}^2\)
    • Centrifugal acceleration: \(\Omega_E^2 R_E \approx 8.5 \times 10^{-4} \text{ m/s}^2\)
    • Coriolis acceleration: \(2 \Omega_E v^{(3)}_{P/E}\) (negligible if \(v^{(3)}_{P/E} \ll 10^{-1} \text{ m/s}\))

3 Spherical Coordinates and Vector Projections

We describe point \(P\) in spherical coordinates \((r, \varphi, \Delta\theta)\) stationary with respect to frame \((3)\), where \(\varphi\) is the colatitude (angle from \(\hat{\mathbf{z}}_3 = \hat{\mathbf{z}}_2\)) and \(\Delta\theta\) is the longitude relative to \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_3\).

3.1 Local Orthonormal Basis \((\hat{\mathbf{R}}, \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}, \hat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}})\)

\[ \begin{cases} \hat{\mathbf{R}} = \hat{\mathbf{z}}_3 \cos\varphi + \hat{\mathbf{x}}_3 \sin\varphi \cos\Delta\theta + \hat{\mathbf{y}}_3 \sin\varphi \sin\Delta\theta \\ \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}} = -\hat{\mathbf{x}}_3 \sin\Delta\theta + \hat{\mathbf{y}}_3 \cos\Delta\theta \\ \hat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}} = -\hat{\mathbf{z}}_3 \sin\varphi + \hat{\mathbf{x}}_3 \cos\varphi \cos\Delta\theta + \hat{\mathbf{y}}_3 \cos\varphi \sin\Delta\theta \end{cases} \]

3.2 Centrifugal Term Evaluation

With \(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{3/0} = \Omega_E \hat{\mathbf{z}}_2\) and \(\mathbf{r}_{EP} = R_E \hat{\mathbf{R}}_P\): \[ \begin{aligned} -\boldsymbol{\omega}_{3/0} \times (\boldsymbol{\omega}_{3/0} \times \mathbf{r}_{EP}) &= -\Omega_E \hat{\mathbf{z}}_2 \times \left[ \Omega_E \hat{\mathbf{z}}_2 \times R_E \left( \hat{\mathbf{z}}_2 \cos\varphi + \hat{\mathbf{x}}_2 \sin\varphi \cos(\theta_3 + \Delta\theta) + \hat{\mathbf{y}}_2 \sin\varphi \sin(\theta_3 + \Delta\theta) \right) \right] \\ &= +R_E \Omega_E^2 \sin\varphi \, \hat{\rho}_P \end{aligned} \] where \(\hat{\rho}_P = \hat{\mathbf{x}}_3 \cos\Delta\theta + \hat{\mathbf{y}}_3 \sin\Delta\theta = \hat{\mathbf{R}} \sin\varphi + \hat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}} \cos\varphi\). Thus: \[ -\boldsymbol{\omega}_{3/0} \times (\boldsymbol{\omega}_{3/0} \times \mathbf{r}_{EP}) = R_E \Omega_E^2 \left( \sin^2\varphi \, \hat{\mathbf{R}} + \sin\varphi \cos\varphi \, \hat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}} \right) \]

3.3 Tidal Term Projection (\(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 \cdot \hat{\mathbf{R}}_P\))

Expressing \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1\) in frame \((2)\): \[ \hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 = (\hat{\mathbf{x}}_2 \cos\varepsilon - \hat{\mathbf{z}}_2 \sin\varepsilon)\cos\theta_1 + \hat{\mathbf{y}}_2 \sin\theta_1 \] Taking the dot product with \(\hat{\mathbf{R}}_P = \hat{\mathbf{z}}_2 \cos\varphi + \hat{\mathbf{x}}_2 \sin\varphi \cos(\theta_3 + \Delta\theta) + \hat{\mathbf{y}}_2 \sin\varphi \sin(\theta_3 + \Delta\theta)\): \[ \hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 \cdot \hat{\mathbf{R}}_P = \cos\varepsilon \cos\theta_1 \sin\varphi \cos(\theta_3 + \Delta\theta) + \sin\theta_1 \sin\varphi \sin(\theta_3 + \Delta\theta) - \sin\varepsilon \cos\theta_1 \cos\varphi \]

(Note: If the obliquity \(\varepsilon = 0\), this simplifies to \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 \cdot \hat{\mathbf{R}}_P = \sin\varphi \cos(\theta_3 + \Delta\theta - \theta_1)\).)

We can decompose \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1\) in the local basis as: \[ \hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 = (\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 \cdot \hat{\mathbf{R}})\hat{\mathbf{R}} + (\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 \cdot \hat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}})\hat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}} + (\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 \cdot \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \] where: * \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 \cdot \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}} = -\cos\varepsilon \cos\theta_1 \sin(\theta_3 + \Delta\theta) + \sin\theta_1 \cos(\theta_3 + \Delta\theta)\) * \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 \cdot \hat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}} = \cos\varepsilon \cos\theta_1 \cos\varphi \cos(\theta_3 + \Delta\theta) + \sin\theta_1 \cos\varphi \sin(\theta_3 + \Delta\theta) + \sin\varepsilon \cos\theta_1 \sin\varphi\)

4 Hydrostatic Equilibrium and Free Surface Elevation (\(\zeta\))

Assuming a fluid particle is approximately at rest with respect to the Earth (\(\mathbf{a}^{(3)}_{P/E} \approx \mathbf{0}\), \(\mathbf{v}^{(3)}_{P/E} \approx \mathbf{0}\)) and \(\frac{\mathbf{F}_{\text{non-g}}}{m} = -\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla P\):

4.1 Component Equations

  1. Radial direction (\(\hat{\mathbf{R}}\)): \[ 0 = -g^E_r - g^M_r + 3\frac{G M_M}{R_{EM}^2} \frac{r}{R_{EM}} (\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 \cdot \hat{\mathbf{R}}_P)^2 + r \Omega_E^2 \sin^2\varphi - \frac{1}{\rho}\partial_r P \] where \(g^E_r = \frac{G M_E}{r^2}\) and \(g^M_r = \frac{G M_M}{R_{EM}^2}\frac{r}{R_{EM}}\).

  2. Azimuthal direction (\(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\)): \[ \frac{1}{\rho r \sin\varphi} \partial_\theta P = g_{\theta, \text{tidal}} \]

  3. Polar/Colatitude direction (\(\hat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\)): \[ \frac{1}{\rho r} \partial_\varphi P = g_{\varphi, \text{tidal}} + r \Omega_E^2 \sin\varphi \cos\varphi \]

4.2 Radial Integration

Let \(r_0\) be the unperturbed spherical radius and \(\zeta(\varphi, \theta_3 + \Delta\theta)\) be the free surface elevation. Integrating the radial equation from \(r = r_0\) to \(r = r_0 + \zeta\) (assuming \(\zeta \ll r_0\)):

  • Earth’s gravity term: \[ -\int_{r_0}^{r_0+\zeta} \frac{G M_E}{r^2} dr = \left. \frac{G M_E}{r} \right|_{r_0}^{r_0+\zeta} = G M_E \left( \frac{1}{r_0 + \zeta} - \frac{1}{r_0} \right) \approx -\frac{G M_E}{r_0^2}\zeta \]

  • Tidal term: \[ -\int_{r_0}^{r_0+\zeta} \frac{G M_M}{R_{EM}^3} r \left( 1 - 3(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 \cdot \hat{\mathbf{R}}_P)^2 \right) dr \approx -\frac{G M_M}{R_{EM}^3} \left( 1 - 3(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 \cdot \hat{\mathbf{R}}_P)^2 \right) r_0 \zeta \]

Combining the integrated terms gives the pressure difference across the layer: \[ \frac{1}{\rho} \int_{r_0}^{r_0+\zeta} \partial_r P \, dr = \frac{P_{\text{atm}} - P(r_0, \mathbf{x})}{\rho} = f(\mathbf{x})\zeta \]

4.3 Boundary Condition on the Free Surface

On the free surface \(r_s = r_0 + \zeta(\varphi, \theta)\), the pressure is constant: \(P(r_0 + \zeta, \varphi, \theta) = P_{\text{atm}}\). Taking total derivatives along the surface: \[ \left.\frac{\partial P}{\partial r}\right|_{r_s} \frac{\partial \zeta}{\partial \varphi} + \left.\frac{\partial P}{\partial \varphi}\right|_{r_s} = 0, \qquad \left.\frac{\partial P}{\partial r}\right|_{r_s} \frac{\partial \zeta}{\partial \theta} + \left.\frac{\partial P}{\partial \theta}\right|_{r_s} = 0 \]

Using the first-order hydrostatic approximation \(\left.\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial P}{\partial r}\right|_{r_s} \approx -\frac{G M_E}{r_0^2}\): \[ \begin{cases} \dfrac{\partial \zeta}{\partial \theta} \approx \dfrac{r_0^3}{G M_E} \, g_{\theta, \text{tidal}} \sin\varphi \\[10pt] \dfrac{\partial \zeta}{\partial \varphi} \approx \dfrac{r_0^3}{G M_E} \left( g_{\varphi, \text{tidal}} + r_0 \Omega_E^2 \sin\varphi \cos\varphi \right) \end{cases} \]

4.4 Equilibrium Tide Solution

Recognizing that the tidal accelerations derive from a potential proportional to \(|\hat{\mathbf{R}}_P \cdot \hat{\mathbf{x}}_1|^2\), these partial differential equations become: \[ \begin{aligned} 0 &= \frac{\partial}{\partial \theta} \left\{ -\zeta + \frac{r_0^4}{G M_E} \frac{M_M}{R_{EM}^3} \frac{3}{2} |\hat{\mathbf{R}}_P \cdot \hat{\mathbf{x}}_1|^2 + \dots \right\} \\ 0 &= \frac{\partial}{\partial \varphi} \left\{ -\zeta + \frac{r_0^4}{G M_E} \frac{M_M}{R_{EM}^3} \frac{3}{2} |\hat{\mathbf{R}}_P \cdot \hat{\mathbf{x}}_1|^2 + \frac{1}{2} \frac{r_0^4 \Omega_E^2}{G M_E} \sin^2\varphi \right\} \end{aligned} \]

Integrating yields the equilibrium surface elevation \(\zeta(\theta, \varphi)\) (including both the lunar tidal bulge and the rotational equatorial bulge): \[ \zeta(\theta, \varphi) = \zeta_0 + \frac{3}{2} \frac{r_0^4}{R_{EM}^3} \frac{M_M}{M_E} |\hat{\mathbf{R}}_P \cdot \hat{\mathbf{x}}_1|^2(\theta, \varphi) + \frac{1}{2} \frac{r_0^4 \Omega_E^2}{G M_E} \sin^2\varphi \]
